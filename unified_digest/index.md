## Research Papers

1. [Vector Policy Optimization: Training for Diversity Improves Test-Time Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22817v1) - 6.85/10
   - Source: arxiv
   - Why: As inference-time search becomes more common in LLM applications, this work highlights the importance of training for diversity rather than optimizing a single scalar reward, potentially changing how post-training is done.
2. [GraphReview: Scientific Paper Evaluation via LLM-Based Graph Message Passing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27204v1) - 6.83/10
   - Source: arxiv
   - Why: It provides a unified framework for scientific paper evaluation that leverages both LLM reasoning and graph structure, potentially improving peer review processes and automated quality assessment.
3. [Bilevel Optimization over Saddle Points of Zero-Sum Markov Games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26654v1) - 6.71/10
   - Source: arxiv
   - Why: This work extends bilevel RL to competitive multi-agent settings, which is crucial for applications like incentive design, mechanism design, and multi-agent RL. The proposed method is practical (first-order, no hypergradients) and comes with strong theoretical guarantees, making it a significant step toward scalable bilevel optimization in games.
4. [Pair-In, Pair-Out: Latent Multi-Token Prediction for Efficient LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27255v1) - 6.68/10
   - Source: arxiv
   - Why: This work addresses the growing inference cost of long chain-of-thought reasoning by jointly optimizing input and output efficiency, removing the verifier overhead that plagues speculative decoding. It offers a practical, training-efficient solution that could make LLMs more deployable in latency-sensitive applications.
5. [From Scores to Gibbs Correctors: Accelerating Uniform-Rate Discrete Diffusion Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27352v1) - 6.59/10
   - Source: arxiv
   - Why: This work provides the first polylog complexity guarantee for discrete diffusion samplers, addressing a key bottleneck in sampling speed. The Gibbs corrector framework is elegant and practical, potentially enabling faster generation in text and symbolic domains without additional training overhead.
6. [A PAC-Bayesian View of Generalisation for Physics-Informed Machine Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26341v1) - 6.59/10
   - Source: arxiv
   - Why: Bridges a theoretical gap in PIML generalization, offering principled guarantees that can guide algorithm design and model selection. The framework is general and could impact other areas combining physics and learning.
7. [Learning to Balance Motor Thermal Safety and Quadrupedal Locomotion Performance with Residual Policy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27046v1) - 6.59/10
   - Source: arxiv
   - Why: Motor overheating is a critical bottleneck for long-duration legged locomotion, especially under payload. This work provides a practical RL-based solution that can be integrated into existing locomotion policies, potentially enabling longer missions for quadruped robots in real-world applications.
8. [OSMa-Bench++: Toward Open-Ended Benchmarking of Semantic Mapping for Manipulation with Prompt-Generated Synthetic Scenes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26831v1) - 6.58/10
   - Source: arxiv
   - Why: It addresses the need for more controllable and extensible benchmarking of semantic mapping for robotic manipulation, enabling systematic evaluation of corner cases that are underrepresented in existing datasets.
